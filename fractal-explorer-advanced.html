Nothing here is a mesh. A shader walks light rays through the fractal's own mathematics and stops where they land, so the detail never runs out and every slider reshapes the structure itself. Drag the depth up and watch new levels bloom; bend the scale away from 2.00 and the pyramid fattens or starves; twist makes it corkscrew through its own levels. Color comes from how deep inside the structure each point lives, like the rainbow print that inspired this. The workshop sliders reshape the math itself: Lean grows it crooked, Bulge fattens or erodes it, Squash flattens or stretches it, Tumble braids the levels sideways, and Morph melts one fractal into another. Light angle swings the sun, fog depth swallows the deep levels, edge glow lights the thin struts from inside, color by level bands the rainbow level by level like the print, and stripe wraps it in contour rings. Resolution trades speed for sharpness: Auto keeps it smooth on any device, and the fixed settings supersample the render so edges come out carved instead of soft. Export STL carves the exact shape on screen into a real mesh using the same mathematics, ready for a slicer and a printer. Higher export detail means a finer mesh and a longer wait. The Form Lab bends the space the fractal lives in: sine waves warp and flute it, level pulse makes every level breathe at a different size, noise bends it off true, gyroid and worley turn it to lattice and honeycomb, sculpt adds or carves an orb, shell hollows it into a wall, and radial symmetry, repetition, and inversion rebuild its whole arrangement. Every Form Lab tool starts at off, so nothing changes until you invite it in. Living form sets the waves in motion. The STL exporter reads the same Form Lab settings, so what you print is what you see. Exports now carve at up to 224 resolution with an even, smoothed surface: take the GLB into Nomad Sculpt on the iPad for the friendliest editing, or the STL to any slicer. High detail takes a while to carve; that patience is the mesh being built to last. The Advanced bench grows the structure before it skins it: branching angle, taper, leader growth, pruning, and seeded wobble shape the recursive skeleton, then geometric overlays snap, groove, panel, wrap, and layer the surface. Morphing can run by height, through a third form, or through an arc, and every one of those choices is mirrored in the exporter, so the GLB matches the view. Scene export can stand the form on the ground, repeat it into rows, rings, and grids, write growth and morph series, roll seeded variants, and hide the settings inside the GLB itself, so any file can return here later and rebuild the shape it came from.

A word on exporting: the mesh is built from the skin you see, and a few settings can carve that skin away entirely, which is when an export reports that nothing is in range. If that happens, ease these off first: Bulge pulled far back (it erodes the struts), Shell wall and Onion layers (they keep only a thin wall), Prune and the orb cutters (they remove whole regions), Flatten base set higher than the top of the form, Pattern B set to Only where both agree at a high amount, fractal Scale pushed far from 2.00, and Depth set very low. Thin features also need enough Export detail to land between mesh points, so raising Export detail rescues shells, rails and grooves that vanish at low settings.
